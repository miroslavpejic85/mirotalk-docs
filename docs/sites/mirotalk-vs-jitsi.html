Honest guidance

Which platform should you choose?

Choose MiroTalk when...

You want multiple purpose-built WebRTC products, a direct commercial license for eligible private use, documented per-product integration, or the ability to select P2P, SFU, and broadcast architectures independently.

Choose Jitsi Meet when...

Your team already understands the Jitsi stack, values its long-established community, needs a Jitsi-specific integration, or wants to use the public service or JaaS rather than evaluate another ecosystem.

Frequently asked

MiroTalk vs Jitsi questions

Is MiroTalk a Jitsi alternative?

Yes. Both support browser-based WebRTC communication and self-hosting. MiroTalk differs by offering separate products for group meetings, P2P rooms, two-person calls, click-to-call, broadcasting, scheduling, and infrastructure administration.

Which is easier to customize?

That depends on your team and workflow. MiroTalk's focused repositories may be easier when your use case closely matches one product. Jitsi has an established developer ecosystem but its complete deployment spans several components.

Can I white-label MiroTalk?

MiroTalk publishes commercial licensing options that include rebranding for eligible end products. Review the official licensing page and controlling marketplace terms before changing branding or distributing a product.

Is self-hosting free?

Source availability does not eliminate infrastructure costs. Both platforms require servers, networking, TLS, monitoring, upgrades, and often TURN capacity. SFU workloads also consume media-server CPU and bandwidth.
